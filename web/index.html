Methodology

Delta WR & tiers

Map win rate minus the hero's baseline win rate in that rank/region/patch slice, averaged across every slice collected. It's a trimmed average: each pair's highest 30% and lowest 30% of slices are set aside, so a few wild slices (one put Lifeweaver at +43% on King's Row, where most sit near −2%) can't drag the number around. S ≥ 3.0%, A 1.2–2.9%, B −1.1–1.1%, C −3.0–−1.3%, D < −3.0%.

Adjusted for noise. Part of every average is noise, since its slices disagree with each other. So each number is pulled toward the hero's overall mean, more strongly the noisier it is: a well-measured +5% stays near +5%, while a +5% from a handful of inconsistent slices may end up near +1%. Without this, thin numbers would land in S or D by chance. How strongly depends on the hero's map sensitivity (below), which a single rank, region or the current patch alone is too thin to measure, so it always comes from all ranks and regions. The tier, archetype and pool picks use the adjusted value; tap a row to see the unadjusted average.

Presence (pick rate lift)

How much more or less often the hero is picked on this map than on their average map, in percent: +30% means picked 30% more often than usual. It's relative so heroes compare fairly: one pick-rate point is a lot for a 3% hero and nothing for a 40% one. Map Priority ≥ +50%, Popular +20 to +49%, Standard within ±20%, Niche ≤ −20%. The lines sit at the same multiples of the typical lift (±33%) as the tier lines do of the typical win rate effect.

Archetypes

Tier crossed with presence, no extra cutoffs of their own. Strong maps (tier A or S): Meta if picked more than usual (Popular or Map Priority), Specialist if picked less (Niche), Favorable otherwise. Weak maps (tier C or D): Trap Pick if picked more than usual, Deterrent if picked less, Unfavorable otherwise. Tier B: Neutral.

Map sensitivity (sigma)

How much a hero's win rate really varies from map to map: the standard deviation of their map averages, minus the part that's just sampling noise. Map-Agnostic < 1.2% (most maps stay within the B band), Flexible 1.2–2.9%, Map-Dependent ≥ 3.0% (a third or more of maps reach S or D). It's measured on all ranks and regions and used for every filter. Each hero's sigma is estimated together with all the others: a hero with clear evidence (like Widowmaker) decides almost alone, while a hero whose map differences are close to the noise (like Sierra) leans toward what's typical for heroes, instead of being treated as having no map effect at all. A hero's class is marked borderline when there's less than a 75% chance their true sigma is in it (the same line as High confidence): they sit close enough to a class line that the data can't tell which side. In the pool's sigma row these show with an asterisk. New and reworked heroes show "too early to tell" until they have history.

Minimum sample size

A hero-map pair needs at least 3 qualifying season/tier/region slices before it's shown at all — otherwise one unlucky slice (a hero with barely any games on a map, all of them losses) can swing the average by dozens of points. Narrowing to a specific rank or region thins the slices out fast, so a hero can disappear from a filtered view even though it shows up on "All ranks / All regions". That's the filter working as intended, not missing data. The exception is a hero or map that hasn't existed long enough to ever reach 3 slices under a narrow filter (a brand-new hero, or a map reworked this season) — those show with whatever data actually exists rather than being hidden outright.

Provisional

Data is collected daily from Blizzard's and Nexon's official stats. The patch in progress (a season or its midseason half) is left out of the averages for its first 21 days, since early-patch numbers are thin and swing a lot. Heroes and maps that only exist in the current patch (new releases, reworks) are shown anyway, built from current-patch data alone and marked Provisional. Expect those to move. After 21 days the patch counts like any other.

Confidence

The chance that the shown tier is the right one for this hero on this map, from the same noise model as the adjustment (how much the slices behind the number disagree, and how big this hero's real map effects are). High: at least 75% (3 in 4). Medium: 50–74%, more likely right than wrong. Low: under 50%, more likely wrong than right; shown faded. A labels tend to be the least certain because the A band is narrow. These probabilities were checked against held-out data: they're off by about 1 point on average on all ranks and regions, 2 on a single rank or region, and 4 on a single rank and region. n is the number of slices; tap a row for the unadjusted average and its 95% range.

Rank and region

Does a hero's map affinity differ between Masters and GM+, or between regions? Each hero-map pair's slices were split into two independent halves (season first halves vs midseasons): a real difference would show up in both halves, noise wouldn't. Between Masters and GM+ nothing repeats, so the old "Better in GM+ / Masters" flags (a third of all pairs carried one) were noise and have been removed. Europe shows a small but consistent difference from the other regions (about 1 point for a typical hero-map pair); the other regions show none. Even for GM+ or European numbers, the all-ranks, all-regions view predicts held-out data better than the narrower view, so it's the most reliable one for everyone. The test and its pass rule were fixed in advance and are rerun every season.

This patch only

The Data filter can switch to the current patch on its own, for a look at how maps play in the live meta. It has far fewer slices per number than the all-seasons view (at most 8, or 1 once you pick a single rank and region), so expect wide confidence ranges.
